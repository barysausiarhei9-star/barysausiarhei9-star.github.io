Once More on the Evolution of the Universe

Elena Berezina

The most popular cosmological theory at present, the “Big Bang”, is not entirely satisfactory. The Pope praised it for being fully consistent with Church doctrine: there was nothing, and then God brought about that very “Big Bang”.

But for a true scientist, God is “persona non grata” in the sense that, even if the existence of a Creator is accepted, science immediately faces the question of what this God actually is and where he came from. In other words, the concept of God as an unknowable entity is a dead end for science. Nevertheless, the “Big Bang” theory cannot answer the question of where this colossal energy ultimately came from.

After the Bang, however, literally from the very first milliseconds, it is more or less clear how the Universe acquired its structure. There is nothing supernatural about it: entropy increases overall, but owing to the energy acquired at the moment of the explosion, the system departs from equilibrium and, developing nonlinearly, gives rise to self-organizing “subsystems”—from atoms and galaxies to living organisms, including thinking ones.

Yet the very beginning can be explained only as follows: the Universe and the Anti-Universe exist eternally in the world. This is not a static state, but their periodic emergence, development, and disappearance at moments corresponding to the “Big Bang/Big Crunch”.

Quantum mechanics describes a similar process of the continual appearance and disappearance (annihilation) of virtual “particle-antiparticle” pairs. Since energy cannot be created out of nothing, one partner has positive energy and the other negative energy, Hawking notes.

The same is true of the “Universe–Anti-Universe” system. It is precisely the force of attraction between these two worlds that gives rise to gravity, which “draws together” all objects and curves space and time, ultimately leading to the inevitable “Big Crunch” at the end of each cycle, in accordance with Friedmann’s first model. But this is not the end of everything, merely a new beginning, since the energy of annihilation once again allows the Universe—and the Anti-Universe—to develop in a new cycle, and so on “ad infinito”.

Perhaps such an idea, “lying on the surface”, has occurred to scientists more than once and been dismissed for the simple reason that the presence of antimatter objects in the cosmos has never been confirmed by astronomers. Antiparticles, meanwhile, are exclusively the province of quantum mechanics.

What is the reason for this state of affairs?

I would like to remind the reader that we can observe only celestial bodies that either emit their own light or reflect light. Suppose, however, that some cosmic object moves faster than light. In that case, a light wave cannot catch up with it and therefore cannot be reflected from it. Such an object cannot, in principle, be detected by astronomers: it will always be in the “reverse cone” of light.

It is known that a material body cannot reach the speed of light, still less exceed it. But as for the category of antimatter… why not? After all, if mass is a measure of inertia, perhaps “antimass” has precisely the opposite properties and is a kind of “measure of mobility”?

When studying objects of quantum mechanics, however, the researcher is, as it were, “outside the system” and can sometimes also “peek” into the reverse light cone. Might this explain a property such as particle spin, whereby a particle looks different in different directions (rather like… the phases of the Moon)?

Quantum mechanics tells us that there is a special relationship between matter and antimatter, spatial symmetry, and the two directions of time. It consists in the following: if particles are replaced with antiparticles, spatial coordinates with their mirror images, and the direction of time is reversed—from the future to the past—the physical laws governing such an object remain unchanged.

Similar relationships apparently exist within the “Universe–Anti-Universe” pair: the Anti-Universe consists of antimatter, has negative energy, is a “mirror image” of our Universe, and exists backward in time, ensuring that all physical laws are preserved within the system. The total sum of matter and energy remains unchanged and equal to zero, in accordance with the first law of thermodynamics. The second law of thermodynamics, concerning increasing entropy, is also, of course, valid—for the material Universe, where irreversible processes occur, characterized by the positive direction of time, from the past to the future.

In the Anti-Universe, however, where time runs backward, entropy, being a function of time, gradually decreases, thus maintaining the perfect symmetry—and stability—of all the system’s parameters at every moment.

Thus, time, which stops at the moment of the “Big Bang”, has two directions as one moves away from that moment, extending both ways from zero, like spatial coordinates: time is positive in the Universe and negative in the Anti-Universe.

The curvature of space acquires a “negative” value in the Anti-Universe, where space is, as it were, “turned inside out”, curving not like the surface of a sphere but like a saddle surface—or, if you prefer, like the inner surface of a sphere, a mirror image of the configuration of the Universe. Interestingly, it is precisely such “saddle-shaped” curvature of space that, according to a number of authors, is required for travel backward in time. Only objects moving faster than light travel into the past—that is, when an object can “overtake itself”. By contrast, objects capable of reaching the speed of light, or a speed close to it, can “catch up with themselves”. For them, time, as it were, stops; they “double”, acquiring the ability to be in two places at once. This is precisely what happens to particles in the double-slit experiment when they begin to exhibit wave properties.

Acknowledging the existence of the Anti-Universe makes it possible to resolve virtually all the unsolved questions of modern cosmology, in particular to locate the “disappeared” antiparticles, which evidently never disappeared at all (indeed, that would contradict the first law of thermodynamics). They merely combined with their own kind into increasingly complex assemblies, following the example of their material partners, and, acquiring the corresponding speed, vanished… from our field of view.

In conclusion, I would also like to note that the entropy of the Anti-Universe, like its energy in general, apparently has a negative sign. This is that very “negentropy”, or, equivalently, information according to Shannon.

What, then? Cosmic fields of information? A Higher Intelligence, perhaps? The indestructibility of our consciousness, which is nothing other than the totality of our knowledge about ourselves and the world around us—in other words, information? Perhaps the immortality of our soul? I do not know. I would only repeat after Spinoza: “God is Nature.” There is no point in looking for anything outside it.
