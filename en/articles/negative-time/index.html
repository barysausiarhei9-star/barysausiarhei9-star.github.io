Negative Time

Elena Berezina

NEGATIVE TIME

It is indeed a dreadful crime

Not to admit the backward time.

A person sees only the light that has reached the retina of their eye.

Any flash of light always occurs before it is seen, since photons need a certain amount of time to reach the observer.

Thus, the movement of photons, and indeed of all objects approaching the observer, proceeds "from earlier to later", from past to future - i.e. in ordinary, forward-flowing, positive time.

Conversely, both photons and other objects moving away from the observer move backwards, back in time. For example, at any given moment, any celestial body is in an ever deeper past relative to the observer the farther it is from earth.

Our organs of sight cannot register photons moving away (not striking the retina).

Yet any item (object) that finds itself in a photon stream may be capable of reflecting such receding photons and, albeit with a delay relative to the initial flash, redirecting them back towards the observer.

And then we shall see these photons , but now reflected, with a certain delay indicating the negative increment that has occurred in their movement through time.

If the object’s velocity is below the speed of light, the photons will catch up with it quite soon. The object will become a source of reflected light and, as such, will sooner or later be registered by the observer. Its time, having slowed somewhat, will remain positive.

If the velocity of movement equals the speed of light, the negative increment cancels out the entire positive passage of time. Time will "zero out", stop, demonstrating the object’s manifestation of wave properties - the ability to be in several (many) places synchronously. All "massless", energy-carrying particles possess precisely this velocity.

Among them are both visible particles and "invisible ones".

And finally, for an object with a velocity exceeding the speed of light, time is completely inverted - directed from future to past, it takes on NEGATIVE values. Objects of negative time proper are entirely inaccessible to visual observation.
