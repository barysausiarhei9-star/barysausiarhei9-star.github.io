MOVEMENT

Elena Berezina

Movement away from an observer means only that an object is moving with a “negative increment” of time.

For a “cosmonaut” traveling at subluminal speed, time WILL REMAIN POSITIVE, but it will SLOW DOWN (a well-known phenomenon). The cosmonaut will “grow younger”)).

IF the cosmonaut could, while moving away, attain the SPEED OF LIGHT (but being MATERIAL, with positive rest mass, he cannot: mass is a measure of inertia), then his time WOULD stop. For tiny particles, however, this is possible. Upon reaching the speed of light, they turn into a wave, “double” (the double-slit experiment). Time coming to a halt means that the object IS IN TWO PLACES AT ONCE (the difference between a wave and a particle, which occupies a definite place at a definite moment in time).

But only when the object's speed becomes FASTER THAN LIGHT DOES TIME BECOME DISTORTED, beginning to flow from the future into the past. IT BECOMES NEGATIVE.

And it is precisely in negative time that ANTIMATTER lives (in accordance with the principle of CPT symmetry, well known to all the physicists in Geneva who have studied antihydrogen).

Russian original: Samlib.
