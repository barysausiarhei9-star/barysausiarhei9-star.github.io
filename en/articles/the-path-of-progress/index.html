The Path of Progress

Elena Berezina

In this essay, Elena Berezina presents her own speculative interpretation of relativity and the twin paradox. The arguments and quotations below are presented as the author’s, rather than as statements of established physics.

 THE PATH OF PROGRESS

 

 “The only path of progress is to refute the existing theory

 and create an alternative”.

 R. Feynman

 

 

 The main (inert) mass of today’s physicists prefers to consider the twin paradox resolved.

 But, as the saying goes, “in reality, nothing is as it actually is”.

 The twin paradox is not merely unresolved: in principle, it cannot be resolved within the constraints imposed by the conservatives, who are bent on turning it into an insoluble problem for the theory of relativity as a whole.

 What, then, is the essence of the paradox?

 The twin paradox is a thought experiment involving two brothers. One twin sets off on a space voyage at near-light velocity and, at its conclusion, returns to earth.

 According to the relativistic effect, the traveller can just as well consider that it is his stay-at-home brother who is moving, rather than himself; each twin will observe the other’s clock slowing down compared with his own.

 Nevertheless, according to special relativity, the brother who flew away and returned will be younger than his twin.

 This is where the paradox arises: why do both observe time slowing down, yet in the end it is the traveller who turns out to be younger than the stay-at-home?

 The generally accepted “solution” to the paradox boils down to the claim that the traveller’s accelerations must not be disregarded: his frame of reference is not inertial, and consequently there is no symmetry in their displacements.

 But is that really so?

 To begin with, inertial forces or gravitational fields ought not to be taken into account, if only because the “ideal”, near-light-velocity brothers in the thought experiment have no mass.

 But that is not so important. More important is that simply extrapolating ONE’S OWN movement onto one’s brother’s displacements cannot, in principle, selectively alter individual details of that movement.

 When the astronaut accelerates relative to the other twin along certain stretches of the journey, his acceleration must be just as relative as his velocity. That is, if the travelling twin recedes, approaches, rests, decelerates or accelerates relative to his brother, then the stay-at-home brother likewise recedes, approaches, rests, decelerates and accelerates relative to the traveller.

 Every feature of both twins’ trajectories in space and time is perfectly symmetrical FROM EACH OTHER’S POINT OF VIEW, just as the principle of relativity requires.

 

 That is, symmetry is not broken, and there should be no paradox.

 The “calculations of accelerations and decelerations” offered merely confirm the paradox, by considering the situation from some third, irrelevant point of view in which the earth(-dweller) is at rest while the traveller moves, thereby producing the asymmetry.

 No wonder Einstein lamented, half in jest: “When the mathematicians pounced on my theory, I myself stopped understanding it!”

 Yes, in the frame of an “outside observer” the twins are not on an equal footing, but who gave permission to jump arbitrarily from one frame of reference to another?

 The very term “relativity” implies that what is observed depends on the chosen frame of reference. And the conservative “solvers” are plainly looking for the most convenient vantage point from which to see what they want: “the little carriage will move off, the platform will stay behind”.

 But in the thought experiment there are ONLY two twins, and only their mutual perception of their relative displacements should be taken into account. Apart from the two brothers, there is simply NOTHING! In essence, there is a single movement, justifiably “ascribed”, in relativistic terms, to two different objects. Where, pray, does the asymmetry come from?

 And even if...

 One meticulous philosopher drew attention to an interesting point:

 “it is by no means obvious,” he said, “that stretches of accelerated motion, so brief in duration, can lead to such tectonic chrono-differences”.

 

 But even all this is not the main thing.

 The main thing is that, “by the Hamburg reckoning”—by the true measure of things—it makes absolutely no difference how these hypothetical brothers move, nor even whether the stay-at-home moves at all or may be regarded as provisionally stationary. What is more, their trajectories of movement can be disregarded altogether.

 The main thing is that, in the end, the brothers meet at a single point in space.

 And one of the conclusions-postulates of the theory of relativity states that time must be different for objects situated at different points, and the same for those situated at one and the same point.

 Now there is a paradox to beat all paradoxes!

 How are we to understand the meeting of the “disproportionately rejuvenated” traveller with his “prematurely decrepit” brother in their shared kitchen?

 Why allow such an inexcusable inconsistency with relativity, trying at any price to justify this paradoxical situation through calculations of “accelerations and decelerations”?

 As Einstein ought to have said, “if the calculations contradict the theory, so much the worse for the calculations!”.

 And rightly so: if calculations help explain why paradoxes... occur in reality(!)—so much the worse for them.

 Feynman rightly held that there are no real paradoxes in physics. All paradoxes are merely confusion in our own understanding.

 Clearing up the confusion over the twins is quite simple—by acknowledging the existence of time flowing backwards, from future to past.

 In fact, the slowing down, the CONTRACTION, of time means nothing other than its NEGATIVE INCREMENT; but this arises not as a consequence of the velocity of movement, but because of the DIRECTION OF MOVEMENT AWAY FROM THE OBSERVER.

 

 As we know, an observer registers photons only at the moment they strike the retina of the eye.

 Any emission of photons always occurs before it is seen, since photons need a certain time to reach the observer (“the light of dead stars reaches” us).

 Thus, the movement of photons, and indeed of all objects moving from any flash (“event”) towards the observer (wherever the latter may be—on earth or beyond it), proceeds from past to future—in ordinary positive time, with its absolute values increasing.

 Accordingly, displacement backwards, away from the observer, means, conversely, movement from future to past, ever deeper as the distance from the recipient increases.

 That is, by changing direction in space, the traveller also changes the direction of movement in time.

 Therefore, on a “there-and-back” journey (movement around a loop), the difference between the two brothers’ clock readings, arising and increasing on the traveller’s “outward” leg, diminishes on the way back and vanishes completely at the moment the twins meet.

 Meeting at a single point, they will once again be the same age—and no paradoxes!

 Naturally, a GREATER velocity allows the twins to move apart or, conversely, draw together more quickly.

 Thus, at a GREATER velocity the time gap between the brothers will INCREASE FASTER or, alternatively, DECREASE FASTER.

 Depending on the direction of the traveller’s movement.

 That is, the logical error that led to the paradox lies in taking the magnitude of velocity into account while ignoring the vector of movement.

 Remove the error, and the paradox will disappear!

 

 What is more, this simple and elegant solution does not refute relativity, but merely confirms, refines and improves it, in accordance with Bohr’s correspondence principle.

 

 Anyway, read on; understanding will come later.

 

 For Einstein’s central idea is: "The little platform will move off—the carriage will stay behind"!
