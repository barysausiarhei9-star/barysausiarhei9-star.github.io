A Paradigm for a Pair of “Diggers”

Elena Berezina

Physicists have long been interested in where the antiparticles formed in the primordial universe in accordance with the law of conservation could have gone. Annihilation did not take place, as the very existence of the material world demonstrates.

There can therefore be only one satisfactory answer to this question: if they have “disappeared”, they have disappeared solely from our field of view, and nowhere else.

In fact, cosmic invisibles made of supposedly “lost” antimatter—antipodal copies of familiar celestial bodies—are still alive and well today, in reverse, negative time, inaccessible to us.

What is negative time?

A person sees only light that has reached the retina of their eye.

Any flash of light always occurs before it is seen, since photons need a certain amount of time to reach the observer. Thus, photons and indeed all objects approaching the observer move “from earlier to later”, from the past to the future—that is, in ordinary, forward-flowing, positive time.

It is logical to conclude that objects (photons and others) moving away from the observer, conversely, move in reverse, “backward” in time. For example, at any given moment, the farther a celestial body is from Earth, the deeper in the past it is relative to the observer.

Our eyes cannot detect receding photons that do not reach the retina.

However, any object that finds itself in a stream of photons may be able to reflect these receding photons and redirect them toward the observer, albeit with a delay relative to the initial flash.

Then we will see these photons, but now reflected, with a certain delay indicating the negative increment that has occurred in their motion through time.

If the object’s speed is subluminal, the photons will catch up with it fairly soon, and time, having slowed somewhat, will remain positive. The object will become a source of reflected light and will be detected as such by the observer.

If the speed of motion equals the speed of light, the negative increment cancels out the entire positive course of time. Time is “zeroed out”, stops, demonstrating the object’s wave properties—its ability to be in many places simultaneously (all “massless”, energy-carrying particles have precisely this speed).

Finally, for an object moving faster than light, time is completely inverted: it begins to run from the future to the past, taking on NEGATIVE values. Objects in genuinely negative time are entirely inaccessible to visual observation: travelling at superluminal speeds, they cannot, in principle, be overtaken by photons (regardless of their direction of motion), produce no reflected light, and remain forever invisible to us.

There is no point even trying to “see” the dark matter of the universe. Only indirect evidence will indicate its undoubted presence. Evidently, this is precisely the “disappeared” antimatter. The conclusion is supported by quantum mechanics: antimatter exists in negative time (the rule of CPT symmetry).

Inverted time corresponds to the complete reversibility of entropic processes, which is precisely what ensures the eternal existence of the physical world, turning it into a Cosmic Perpetual-Motion Machine—a cyclic process of the formation, development, and involution of antipodal worlds, from one annihilation Big Bang to the next—“ad infinito”.

If, in a scientific debate, you are armed only with facts and logic, your concept will be rejected.

Unless two or three “diggers” who happen to read it dig down to the heart of the matter.

You can dig, or you can choose not to. Up to you.
