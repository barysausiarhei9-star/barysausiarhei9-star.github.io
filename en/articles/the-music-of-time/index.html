THE MUSIC OF TIME: CRESCENDO AND DIMINUENDO

Elena Berezina

The theory of relativity, strictly speaking, means that time must be different for objects located at different points in the space-time continuum, and the same for objects located at one and the same point.

Thus, the meeting in their shared kitchen between a traveler who has become “disproportionately younger” and his brother who has “grown old before his time” is a rather vexing paradox.

After all, as Feynman rightly observed, the existence of paradoxes merely means that there is something we do not fully understand.

Why, then, tolerate such an unforgivable inconsistency, attempting at all costs to justify the paradoxical situation through calculations of “acceleration and deceleration”?

As Einstein ought to have said, “if calculations contradict the theory, so much the worse for the calculations!”

Quite right: if calculations help explain why paradoxes... occur in reality(!), then so much the worse for them.

And the confusion over the twins can be resolved quite simply: by acknowledging the existence of time flowing backward, from the future into the past.

In fact, the slowing down, the COMPRESSION, of time means nothing other than its NEGATIVE INCREMENT; but this arises not as a consequence of the speed of motion, but because of the DIRECTION OF MOTION AWAY FROM THE OBSERVER.

After all, every emission of photons takes place before an observer can register it: photons need time to travel from the site of the flash (the “event”) to the observer. In other words, in such a case photons travel from the past into the present. Motion in the opposite direction, however—away from the observer—is motion backward in time, from the present into the past.

Thus, during a round-trip journey (motion along a loop), the difference between the two brothers' clock readings, arising and increasing on the traveler's outward journey, diminishes on the return journey and disappears completely when the twins meet.

Meeting again at the same point, they will once more be the same age—and there will be no paradoxes!

Naturally, a GREATER speed allows the twins to move apart or, conversely, draw closer more quickly.

This means that at a GREATER speed, the difference in time between the brothers will grow or diminish MORE QUICKLY.

In other words, the logical error that gave rise to the paradox lies in taking account of the magnitude of velocity while ignoring the direction of motion—the cosmic “time zones”.
