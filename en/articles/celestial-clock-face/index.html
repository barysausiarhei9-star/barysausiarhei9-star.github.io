The Celestial Clock Face

Elena Berezina

Although the cosmos is an entire ocean of light from all kinds of sources, at any given moment Earth is washed by ONE SINGLE, quite specific wave:

the light of a flash that occurred, say, 8 minutes ago at a distance of 8 light-minutes from Earth,

or 5 hours ago at a distance of 5 light-hours,

or a year ago at a distance of one light-year,

or 40 years ago at a distance of 40 light-years,

or a million (billion) years ago at a distance of a million (billion) light-years, and so on, all the way to the edge of the visible universe…

The “crests” of this wave are segments of space and time, inseparably linked and “marked out” by the speed of light, where the distance in kilometres corresponds precisely to the time it takes light to cover that distance in a vacuum.

This is the “celestial clock face”—the natural graduation of the cosmic scale, forming distinctive “time zones” of the universe.

All other “events” are, at any given moment, entirely unobservable to a subluminal observer and therefore completely irrelevant, being in negative time—another, “not our own” dimension of cosmic space.

For rays of the forward light cone, the scale is calibrated with successively increasing absolute values of the reading; in the reverse cone, with decreasing values. The point where the forward and reverse cones meet is the zero point of the scale. Naturally, when the zero point coincides with the observer’s location, the magnitude decreases in every direction of motion away from it.
