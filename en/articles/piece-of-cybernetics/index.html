A PIECE OF CYBERNETICS

Elena Berezina

... I am prepared to agree with R. Ashby: every open dynamic system, if the laws governing it remain constant for a sufficiently long time, gives rise to its own form of intelligent life.

Then the only question is how to identify and delimit the system correctly in order to look for that intelligent life within it. For example, are we surprised that fingers do not grow on ears? Yet their absence from hands is regarded as a deviation from the norm. We must remember that, on cosmological scales, we cannot reliably judge whether we are dealing with an entire system (an “organism”) or merely a part of one. Moreover, we may easily mistake “ears” for “hands”. Therefore, I would not be surprised by the absence of extraterrestrial intelligent life — on Mars, in the Solar System, in our galaxy, or even in the universe as a whole. Nor would I be particularly surprised by its presence in any of those places.
