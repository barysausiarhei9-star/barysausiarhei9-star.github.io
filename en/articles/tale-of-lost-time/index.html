A TALE OF LOST TIME

Elena Berezina

Photons need some time to reach an observer; that is, every emission of particles takes place before it is detected. Thus, the movement of photons (and indeed any movement) from an “event” toward an observer occurs in ordinary positive time — from the past into the present (future), that is, with an increase in the magnitude of positive time values or a decrease in negative ones. Movement away from the observer, on the contrary, occurs in “reverse” time, from the future into the past. Reverse movement through time can occur either with a decrease in the magnitude of positive numbers or with an increase in the magnitude of negative numbers, depending on the chosen coordinate value of the starting point. The speed of movement is only of secondary importance, in the sense that the decrease or increase in the absolute values of time occurs faster at a higher speed. The subluminal speed of an observed object, with a negative increment in magnitude, makes it possible to register only the contraction or slowing of time. At the speed of light (characteristic of energy-carrying particles), the negative increment “cancels out” the entire passage of positive time. Superluminal objects always exist in minus time, that is, in genuinely negative time.

In negative time, antiparticles live invisibly. Researchers studying antihydrogen have reminded us of this once again. Objects consisting of large quantities of antimatter are what is known as “dark matter”.

Negative time means superluminal speeds. Ordinary objects and those composed of antimatter are separated from one another solely by the barrier of speed.

This “antiworld” is visually unobservable to us, but it affects objects in the visible, “material” world, up to the point of annihilation with it at the moment of the Big Bang, which begins a new cycle in the formation of worlds that are opposites of one another, ad infinitum.
