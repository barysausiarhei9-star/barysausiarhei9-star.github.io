TWINS WITHOUT A PARADOX

Elena Berezina

It is indeed a dreadful crime
Not to admit the backward time.

Once Einstein declared: "We are compelled to consider possible a mechanism of signal transmission in which the resulting effect precedes its cause. This result contains no logical contradictions!"

He caught the disapproving murmurs of his colleagues and hastened to add: "However, since all our experience provides no evidence of violations of cause-and-effect relationships, the impossibility of superluminal velocities may be regarded as proven."

Everyone breathed a sigh of relief, and only the obstinate M. Gell-Mann stubbornly muttered:

"Everything that is not forbidden is compulsory!"

Einstein nodded understandingly and spread his hands apologetically.

At that unfortunate moment, unnoticed by anyone, the "Twin Paradox" made its appearance.

"There are no real paradoxes in physics," R. Feynman maintains. "All paradoxes are merely confusion in our own understanding."

The rejection of superluminal velocities (and therefore of negative time), contrary to the logic of the theory of relativity, led to the notion that a twin who makes a journey through space, completes a loop and returns to Earth will be younger than his stay-at-home brother. A paradox considered unresolved to this day.

Yet the solution is remarkably simple.

The human eye registers photons only when they reach the retina.

Any flash of light occurs before it is seen, since photons require a certain amount of time to reach the observer.

Thus, the movement of photons and indeed of all cosmic objects approaching the observer takes place "from earlier to later," from the past into the future — in ordinary positive time, with its absolute values increasing.

Conversely, when moving away from the observer, motion proceeds backward, from the future into an ever more distant past as the distance increases.

In other words, by changing direction in space, the traveler also changes the direction of movement through time.

Therefore, during a round trip, the difference between the clocks of the traveling twin and his stay-at-home brother, arising and increasing on the outward journey, decreases on the return journey and disappears completely when the brothers meet.

Embracing each other, they will once again be the same age, as twins should be.

The logical error that gave rise to the paradox, as we can see, was that the direction of motion was ignored, while only the magnitude of velocity was taken into account.

Unfortunately, negative time flowing backward (from the future into the past) is still far from universally accepted by researchers.

But once it is taken into account — "there was a paradox, and now it's gone."

It should be emphasized that at extreme (relativistic) velocities, the direction of motion is actually irrelevant: for lightlike objects, cause and effect are simultaneous, since, acquiring the properties of a wave, they set off and reach their destination synchronously, as though "multiplying" themselves, being in several places at once.

Superluminal objects exist permanently in negative time, since they always outrun the light wave with which they originally "stood at the same starting line."

They cannot, in principle, be observed directly and reveal themselves only through indirect signs. In negative time, entropic processes are reversible, and consequently the effect precedes the cause.

And please don't say that this refutes the theory of relativity. More precisely, it represents a further development of that theory.
