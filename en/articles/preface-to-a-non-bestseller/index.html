PREFACE TO A NON-BESTSELLER

Elena Berezina

&

All classical theories of physics claimed to provide an objective description of the world, but they did not take into account the observer's influence on the subject of inquiry. The new physics is called upon to overcome this underestimation of the observer's role, without denying the possibility of obtaining objective knowledge.

The necessity of such an approach has been compellingly demonstrated by both the theory of relativity and quantum mechanics.

To preserve objectivity, we must clearly establish the circumstances under which the observer evaluates the information received, understand the causes, and foresee the consequences of the inevitable limitations of our assessment.

Human beings are not capable of perceiving the "symmetrical" world through their senses, but this by no means implies that such a world is unreal: indirect evidence and the logic of things unequivocally point to its existence.

This world is unobservable, yet it affects the objects of the visible, "material" world, even to the point of annihilation with it at the moment of the Big Bang, which marks the beginning of a new cycle in the emergence of worlds that are opposites of one another, ad infinitum.
