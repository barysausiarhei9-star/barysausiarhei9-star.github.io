The Albino Bull

Elena Berezina

Photons require a certain amount of time to reach an observer; that is, any emission of particles occurs before it is detected. Therefore, the motion of photons (and indeed any motion whatsoever) from an "event" toward an observer takes place in ordinary positive time — from the past into the present (future), that is, with an increase in the absolute value of positive time coordinates or a decrease in the absolute value of negative ones. Motion away from the observer, by contrast, takes place in "reverse" time, from the future into the past. Backward motion through time may involve either a decrease in the absolute value of positive numbers or an increase in the absolute value of negative numbers, depending on the chosen coordinate value of the starting point. The speed of motion is of merely secondary importance, in the sense that the decrease or increase in the absolute values of time occurs more rapidly at higher speeds. The subluminal speed of an observed object, when accompanied by a negative increment in the absolute value, allows only the contraction or slowing of time to be registered. At the speed of light (characteristic of energy-carrying particles), the negative increment "cancels out" the entire passage of positive time. Superluminal objects are always in minus time — genuinely negative time.

  In negative time, antiparticles exist invisibly. Researchers studying antihydrogen have reminded us of this once again. Objects consisting of large quantities of antimatter constitute what is known as "dark matter".

  Negative time means superluminal speeds. Ordinary objects and those composed of antimatter are separated from one another solely by the speed barrier. This "antiworld" is visually unobservable to us, yet it affects objects in the visible, "material" world, even to the point of annihilation with that world at the moment of the Big Bang, which marks the beginning of a new cycle in the formation of the universe and its antiworld counterpart, ad infinitum.
