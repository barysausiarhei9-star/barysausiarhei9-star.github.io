## A Simple Solution to the Twin Paradox

Rejecting faster-than-light speeds (and with them negative time) runs counter to the logic of the theory of relativity. That rejection led to the idea that, after making a loop through outer space and returning to Earth, the travelling twin would be younger than his stay-at-home brother. A paradox still regarded as unresolved to this day.

Recognising the reality of negative time, on the other hand, resolves this paradox quite simply. But what exactly is time that runs backwards, from the future into the past?

A person registers photons only when they strike the retina. Any emission of photons takes place before it is seen, since photons need time to reach the observer.

Thus photons, and indeed all objects approaching the observer, move “from earlier to later”, from the past into the future, in ordinary positive time, with its absolute values increasing.

Moving away from the observer, accordingly, means going backwards, from the future into the past, and going deeper into it the farther one gets from the receiver.

In other words, when the traveller changes direction in space, he also changes direction in time.

On a round trip, then, the difference between the traveller’s clock and his stay-at-home twin’s, which first appears and grows on the outward leg, diminishes on the way back and disappears altogether when the twins meet.

The logical error that led to the paradox, as we can see, was to ignore the vector of motion and take only the magnitude of the velocity into account.

Unfortunately, negative time running backwards (from the future into the past) is still a long way from being accepted by all researchers.

That is because, at ordinary speeds below the speed of light, the moving object’s time is “compressed” but remains the familiar, <!-- Source PDF p. 4 / printed p. 2 --> positive time.

At the speed of light, an object sets out and reaches its destination simultaneously. Its time is “zeroed out”. The traveller seems to “multiply”, occupying several places at once and thus acquiring the properties of a wave. As for faster-than-light objects, they are always in negative time, since they are always ahead of the light wave they were “on the same starting line” with at the outset. They cannot in principle be observed visually and make their presence known only indirectly.

## The Clock, Eternal and Flawless

A universal clock, “the Almighty’s Clock”, ought to show not only the positive time we are used to but also negative time, running from the future into the past.

A reminder: a light-year (a unit for measuring cosmic distances) is the distance light travels in a year (about 9.5 trillion kilometres).

A year, accordingly, is the time it takes light to cover that number of kilometres (a distance of one light-year).

That is, cosmic time is measured by the distance light covers during it. And distance, accordingly, by the time light needs to cover it.

When the two are so inseparably linked, is it not natural to expect that time, like space, should be a **vector** with two directions, extending both ways from a chosen zero point on the coordinate axis?

That is exactly how things are in reality: photons from any point source (and point sources are what astrophysicists deal with) head off in **all** directions. In the **forward** light cone the rays travel towards the observer (towards Earth), while in the **reverse** light cone they travel in the opposite direction.

A human being, as a biological creature, can see only light that falls on the retina, light from the **forward** cone.

We do not see light from the reverse cone at all, except by **reflection**. In that case what we see is once again light in the **forward** cone, but from a different, “secondary” light source, even farther from Earth than the first, in time as well as space.

If we take a flash of light as zero on the coordinate axis, spacetime in its forward cone will be positive (which is how we register it). The reverse cone, by contrast, gives us grounds to speak of a negative spacetime continuum inaccessible to observation.

Earth’s coordinate is then positive and corresponds to <!-- Source PDF p. 6 / printed p. 4 --> the distance between Earth and the flash in question.

To generalise, the motion of photons (and of all other objects) **towards the observer**, towards Earth, from any point in the visible universe must **always** be taken as positive. Motion **away from** Earth, away from the observer, is the negative **direction of motion** along the spacetime coordinate axis (motion with a “negative increment” of spacetime, let’s say).

In choosing an origin, of course, we can take absolutely any “event” (as astrophysicists call points in spacetime) as the zero point.

Earth itself is one possibility. To keep the frame of reference in this case, however, **any** point away from Earth (away from the observer) gets a minus sign in front of the absolute value of its distance.

Here I would like to stress once more that, in studying the cosmos, human beings can see **only** sources of light, owing to the way we are built. Whether the light is emitted or reflected does not matter. What matters is this: an object **is registered** only when the photons from the flash it emitted or reflected have travelled the entire distance separating them from Earth. No more and no less.

So although the cosmos is an entire ocean of light from every sort of source, Earth at any given moment is washed by **one single**, quite specific wave. It is made up of light from a flash that occurred, say, 8 minutes ago at a distance of eight light-minutes from Earth, 5 hours ago at a distance of 5 light-hours, a year ago at a distance of one light-year, 40 years ago at a distance of 40 light-years, a million (a billion) years ago at a distance of a million (a billion) light-years, and so on, all the way to the edge of the visible universe.

The “crests” of this wave are intervals of space and time that the speed of light binds “fast” together. They are, so to speak, equivalent: “x” light-years = “x” years. Time is equivalent to space.

All the other flashes, such as those that occurred 7 years ago at a distance of 8 light-years, or the other way round, 8 years ago at a distance of 7 <!-- Source PDF p. 7 / printed p. 5 --> light-years, are all “irrelevant”, so to speak.

Here is why. For convenience, let us put Earth at zero on the coordinate axis. Then the resulting position in space and time of the photons from the first of those “wrong” flashes, relative to Earth, can be written as **−8 + 7 = −1**. From the point minus eight years, the photons have spent seven years drawing closer to Earth. That positive increment was not enough to reach the observer (the zero point).

The coordinates of the second flash are **−7 + 7 + (−1) = −1**. That is because throughout the final, “extra” year of their journey the photons were **moving away** from Earth again, away from the zero point, on the negative-number scale.

In both cases the particles of light end up, relative to Earth, in the unobservable reverse light cone, **in negative time and space**. And the light in the reverse cone, along with the objects in it, is, as we know, invisible to us.

Let us pick any stretch of spacetime separating the observer (Earth) from another point (an event) in outer space, say a stretch of “x” light-years, and have a swift rider on a spirited horse dash from one end to the other. Let his speed be one and a half times the speed of light.

We can put the observer at zero, which puts the starting point at “minus x”. At first, the motion is towards Earth. But after “x” years, when light would just have reached the retina of the eye registering it, point zero, our cosmic wanderer will have travelled so fast that he will be somewhere else entirely:

**−x + x + (−0.5x) = −0.5x**

Once again, the coordinate is negative!

And the result? The poor fellow sailed straight over Earth, like a sheet of plywood over Paris. Nobody even noticed him!

And, characteristically, as he travels away from Earth he will also find himself in the negative cone of an “event” lying “x” years from Earth.

Even when travelling **towards** the light, such an object invariably finds itself in <!-- Source PDF p. 8 / printed p. 6 --> the reverse cone of any **oncoming** flash-event! Picture it: the flash has coordinate zero. Earth is some distance away from it in the positive direction along the axis, “plus x”, let’s say. At some point the rider takes off towards the light. The countdown is on! If he were travelling at the speed of light, he would reach zero in “x” years, the epicentre of the light wave. But no, he’s racing along one and a half times as fast as light! So after “x” years he will be at “minus 0.5x”, in the reverse cone of the flash:

**+x − x + (−0.5x) = −0.5x**

Negative time again! And negative space, of course. As they say, “in the wrong place at the wrong time”. The result: **invisible**! (Note: the actual figures naturally differ from those given here, since light rays and objects with mass follow different trajectories, but the principle remains the same.)

Since “x” can be any natural number at all, the conclusion suggests itself: it is absolutely impossible for us to see anything capable of moving faster than light, because an object with that speed is **permanently** in the reverse light cone. That holds wherever it starts within the visible universe and whichever way it heads.

A faster-than-light object can never be overtaken by light or “collide with it”, and so can never be a source of reflected rays. Consequently it cannot be detected by an observer. Never. Under no circumstances.

All material bodies, on the other hand, are barred even from reaching the speed of light, let alone exceeding it. Sooner or later they are bound to come into view as sources of reflected light.

## The Law of Popularity

Photons take a certain amount of time to reach the observer, so particles are always emitted before the emission is registered. Thus photons go from the “event” to the observer in ordinary positive time, from the past into the future. Motion away from the observer, by contrast, takes place in “reverse” time, from the future into the past. Backwards motion in time can decrease the absolute value of positive numbers or increase that of negative ones, depending on the coordinates of the starting point. Conversely, motion from the event to the observer increases the magnitude of positive values or decreases that of negative ones. This holds for any subluminal observer, wherever they may be. It is evidence both for the isotropy of the universe and for the objective character of the knowledge obtained. Speed plays only a supporting role in the sense that the greater the speed, the more quickly these absolute values of time increase or decrease. If the object under observation is moving below the speed of light and the increment in magnitude is negative, we can register only the compression, the slowing down, of time. At the speed of light (characteristic of energy-carrying particles), however, the negative increment “cancels out” the entire advance of positive time. Faster-than-light objects are in negative time. Rejecting negative time is tantamount to rejecting the theory of relativity. (And that is not permissible even for Einstein himself.) Until physicists acknowledge that negative time exists, come to grips with its nature and recognise the presence of invisible, faster-than-light objects made of antimatter within it, the argument is doomed to remain hopelessly deadlocked.
